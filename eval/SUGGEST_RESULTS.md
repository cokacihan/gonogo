# Where would the REQUIREMENT() line go? (`gonogo suggest`, measured)

150 requirements in 25 F´ components are cited today by test code of their own component. For each, `suggest` ranks the component's test code without reading the citations, and we look for the place where the macro is actually written. Median candidates per requirement: 37 test bodies and helpers, 13 test cases. Generated by `python -m eval.suggest_eval` (101.0 s).

## Cite site (the TEST body or helper function that holds the macro)

| ranking | hit@1 | hit@3 | hit@5 | MRR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| full | 0.44 | 0.65 | 0.73 | 0.56 |
| identifiers | 0.19 | 0.39 | 0.53 | 0.34 |
| random | 0.07 | 0.21 | 0.32 | 0.20 |

## Test case (a case that reaches the macro over CALLS)

| ranking | hit@1 | hit@3 | hit@5 | MRR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| full | 0.62 | 0.85 | 0.89 | 0.74 |
| identifiers | 0.54 | 0.77 | 0.85 | 0.68 |
| no call graph | 0.59 | 0.76 | 0.79 | 0.67 |
| random | 0.24 | 0.50 | 0.64 | 0.41 |

`random` is the exact expected value of a uniformly random order of the same candidates. `identifiers` leaves out the TEST_CASE()/COMMENT() descriptions, which could repeat a requirement's words next to its citation. `no call graph` ranks a case only by its own TEST body. Candidates with equal scores count at their expected position under a random order, so a tie is never broken in favour of the answer; candidates that share no word with the requirement are not ranked and count as misses.

## Do the descriptions give the answer away?

Share of a requirement's words found in the TEST_CASE()/COMMENT() descriptions of a candidate: in 0 of 453 (requirement, answer site) pairs the descriptions carry 80 % or more of them, in 6 pairs 60 % or more (other candidates: 5 and 8 of 10851 pairs). In these requirements the descriptions say what a test checks rather than repeat the requirement sentence. Elsewhere in F´ they sometimes do repeat it (Svc/Health's `faultTlm` writes HTH-003's sentence in a COMMENT next to its near-miss citation ISF-HTH-003, outside this measurement), so the `identifiers` row is reported as the conservative figure.
